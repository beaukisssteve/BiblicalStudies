# Genesis 01 — Creation

**Passage:** Genesis 1:1–2:3 (the seventh day appears in chapter 2).

## What the text explicitly says
- God creates the heavens and the earth ([[Creation]]).
- Day 1: light and darkness (Genesis 1:3–5).
- Day 2: expanse/sky and separation of waters (1:6–8).
- Day 3: dry land, seas, vegetation (1:9–13).
- Day 4: lights for seasons, days, years (1:14–19).
- Day 5: sea creatures and birds (1:20–23).
- Day 6: land animals and humanity, male and female, in the [[Image of God]] (1:24–31).
- Day 7: God ceases His completed work, blesses and sanctifies the day ([[Sabbath]]; 2:1–3).

## Literary observations
- Repeated formulas: “And God said,” “and it was so,” and “God saw that it was good.”
- Days 1–3 establish domains; days 4–6 correspond to them.

## Theological interpretation
- Interpretations of the days differ (ordinary-day, day-age, framework, and other approaches). Distinguish the text's statements from a particular chronology model.
- New Testament theological connections: John 1:1–3; Colossians 1:16–17; Hebrews 11:3.

## Archaeology and external evidence
- Ancient Near Eastern creation texts provide literary and historical context, not archaeological verification of the creation event.
- Evaluate specific comparisons individually and cite editions/translations before drawing conclusions.

## Questions
- How does the text distinguish the Creator from created things?
- What does “image of God” mean in its literary context?
- What does divine rest communicate?

## Sources
- Genesis 1:1–2:3; Exodus 20:8–11; John 1:1–3.
