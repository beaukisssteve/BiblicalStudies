# 31 Obadiah — Bible Survey (Expanded)

> [!summary] Central message
> Pride and violence meet judgment; the kingdom belongs to the LORD.

## Book Information

- **Hebrew name:** עֹבַדְיָה (*Ovadyah — Servant of the LORD*).
- **Authorship:** Obadiah.
- **Historical setting:** Edom and Jerusalem’s disaster; dating debated.
- **Date:** Dates of composition and final editing vary by book and scholarly tradition; consult a study Bible for detailed dating discussions.
- **Canonical position:** Old Testament book 31 of 39.

## Structure and Outline

- 1–14 Edom’s pride
- 15–21 Day of the LORD and Zion’s restoration

### Obadiah 1
Edom’s pride and violence are judged; Zion is restored and the kingdom belongs to the LORD.

## Covenants and Redemptive History

- **Abrahamic covenant:** Consider how the promises of offspring, land, blessing, and blessing to the nations (Genesis 12:1–3; 15; 17) provide background or advance in this book.
- **Mosaic covenant:** Consider Israel’s obligation to worship the LORD, its failures, and the pattern of covenant blessing and judgment (Exodus 19–24; Deuteronomy 28–30).
- **Davidic covenant:** Where kingship or the promised ruler appears, read in light of God’s promise to David (2 Samuel 7:12–16). Do not assume every book directly mentions this covenant.
- **New covenant:** Where forgiveness, renewal, restored worship, or the Spirit appears, compare Jeremiah 31:31–34 and Ezekiel 36:25–27. Such connections may be thematic rather than explicit predictions.

## God’s Character Revealed

- **Sovereignty and faithfulness:** Trace God’s rule and fidelity in Obadiah, especially Obadiah 15.
- **Holiness and justice:** Notice how the LORD evaluates evil, idolatry, and human pride (compare Exodus 34:6–7).
- **Mercy and patience:** Look for God’s provision, forgiveness, and preservation of His people (compare Psalm 103:8–13).

## Major Themes

- Pride and violence meet judgment; the kingdom belongs to the LORD.
- The LORD’s final kingship.
- Covenant loyalty, human need, and God’s unfolding plan of redemption.

## Christ-Centered Reading and Typology

- **Central connection:** The LORD’s final kingship.
- **Reading principle:** Distinguish explicit New Testament identification from a broader canonical analogy. Christ fulfills the Law, Prophets, and Writings (Luke 24:27,44).

## The Big Lesson

Pride and violence meet judgment; the kingdom belongs to the LORD. Read this book as part of the Bible’s unified account of creation, fall, covenant, redemption, and consummation.

## Study Questions

1. What does this book reveal about the LORD’s character?
2. How do human sin and divine mercy appear together?
3. Which promises or patterns point forward to Christ?
4. What response of faith and obedience does this book call for?
