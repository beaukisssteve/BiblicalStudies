# Evidence Standards

Use labels consistently and cite sources with page numbers, publication dates, and URLs or DOIs where available.

- **Biblical text:** What a passage explicitly says. Cite book, chapter, verse, and translation.
- **Theological interpretation:** Inferences from the text; identify the interpretive tradition and alternative readings where relevant.
- **Historical tradition:** Claims found in later Jewish, Christian, or other traditions; name the earliest attested source and date if known.
- **Historical / archaeological evidence:** Physical evidence, inscriptions, manuscripts, and scholarly analyses. Distinguish the observed artifact from the proposed interpretation.

## Evidence status
- **Well established:** Strong independent evidence and broad scholarly agreement.
- **Plausible:** Reasonable support, but not settled.
- **Disputed:** Significant competing interpretations.
- **Unsupported / not yet established:** Evidence is insufficient to affirm the claim.

**Caution:** Archaeology cannot directly test every theological claim. Lack of evidence is not proof of absence. A find confirming a name or place does not automatically verify every narrative connected to it.

## Source record
Record: author; title; publisher/journal; year; page; DOI/URL; date accessed; primary or secondary source; limitations; competing interpretations.
