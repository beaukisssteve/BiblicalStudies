# 📖 **Numbers Survey (Expanded)**

### **Numbers**

- **Hebrew Name:** _Bamidbar_ (בְּמִדְבַּר — “In the wilderness”)
- **Meaning:** “In the wilderness”
- **Author:** Moses
- **Date Written:** ~1445–1405 BC
- **Time Period Covered:** ~40 years (Sinai to plains of Moab)

---

## **Chapter Summary**

1. Census of the first generation.
2. Arrangement of the camp around the tabernacle.
3. Levites set apart for service.
4. Duties of the Levite clans.
5. Laws of purity and restitution.
6. Nazirite vow; priestly blessing.
7. Offerings for dedication of the altar.
8. Levites consecrated.
9. Passover observed; cloud guides Israel.
10. Israel departs Sinai.
11. Complaining about hardship; manna despised.
12. Miriam and Aaron oppose Moses.
13. Spies sent into Canaan.
14. Rebellion after bad report; judgment pronounced.
15. Laws and reminder of obedience (tassels).
16. Korah’s rebellion against leadership.
17. Aaron’s staff buds to confirm priesthood.
18. Duties and provisions for priests and Levites.
19. Red heifer and purification from death.
20. Water from the rock; Moses sins; Aaron dies.
21. Bronze serpent; victories over enemies.
22. Balaam summoned by Balak.
23. Balaam blesses Israel (first oracle).
24. Balaam’s further prophecies (including star prophecy).
25. Israel sins with Moab; judgment falls.
26. Second census (new generation).
27. Daughters of Zelophehad; Joshua appointed.
28. Daily and festival offerings.
29. Additional festival offerings.
30. Laws about vows.
31. War against Midian.
32. Tribes settle east of the Jordan.
33. Journey recounted.
34. Boundaries of the land.
35. Cities of refuge established.
36. Inheritance laws preserved.

---

## **Covenants**

- **Mosaic Covenant (Tested and Broken) — Throughout**
    
    - Repeated disobedience (ch. 11, 14, 16, 25)
    - Demonstrates Israel’s inability to keep covenant faithfully
- **Priestly Covenant — Numbers 25:10–13**
    
    - Given to Phinehas
    - Covenant of “perpetual priesthood” due to zeal for God
- **Abrahamic Covenant (Still Governing)**
    
    - Despite failure, God preserves the people and promises land

---

## **God’s Character Revealed**

- **Patient / Longsuffering** — Endures constant rebellion
- **Just** — Judges unbelief (14; entire generation dies)
- **Holy** — Sin cannot be ignored (Korah, Nadab pattern continues)
- **Faithful** — Keeps promises despite Israel’s failure
- **Sovereign** — Controls nations and even pagan prophets (Balaam)
- **Provider** — Manna, water, protection
- **Disciplining Father** — Trains His people through consequences
- **Merciful** — Provides means of healing (bronze serpent)

---

## **Major Themes**

- Wilderness testing
- Faith vs unbelief
- Judgment and discipline
- God’s faithfulness vs human rebellion
- Leadership and authority
- The danger of complaining
- The preservation of God’s promise
- Transition from old generation to new
- Holiness in the midst of failure

---

## **Key Redemptive Texts**

- **Numbers 14:18** — God slow to anger, yet just
- **Numbers 21:8–9** — Bronze serpent lifted up for healing
- **Numbers 24:17** — “A star shall come out of Jacob”
- **Numbers 25:11–13** — Zeal leading to covenant peace

---

## **Cross-References to NT Fulfillment**

- **Bronze Serpent (Numbers 21:8–9)**  
    → John 3:14–15 (Christ lifted up for salvation)
    
- **Unbelief in Wilderness (Numbers 14)**  
    → Hebrews 3–4 (warning against hardened hearts)
    
- **Balaam’s Prophecy (Numbers 24:17)**  
    → Matthew 2:2 (star associated with the King)
    
- **Complaining and Judgment (Numbers 11, 21)**  
    → 1 Corinthians 10:5–11 (example for believers)
    
- **Priestly Covenant (Numbers 25)**  
    → Hebrews 7 (fulfilled ultimately in Christ’s priesthood)
    

---

## **Typology (Pay attention here—this is sharp)**

- **Bronze Serpent → Christ**
    
    - Lifted up
    - Looking = living
    - Faith-based healing
- **Moses → Christ (with contrast)**
    
    - Mediator
    - But fails (Numbers 20)
    - Points to need for a better mediator
- **Joshua → Christ**
    
    - Leads people into the Promised Land
    - Name itself (Yeshua) = “The Lord saves”
- **Wilderness → Christian Life**
    
    - Between redemption and inheritance
    - Testing ground of faith

## Straight Talk

Numbers is uncomfortable because it’s honest.

Israel:

- Saw miracles
- Heard God’s voice
- Ate heaven’s bread

…and still:

- Complained
- Rebelled
- Refused to trust

And God’s response?

- Judgment, yes
- But also **faithfulness to His promises anyway**

---

## The Big Lesson

👉 Redemption doesn’t automatically produce obedience.

You can be:

- Delivered from Egypt
- Walking with God
- Still deeply prone to unbelief

Which is exactly why the New Testament keeps pointing back to this book as a warning.
